The Kind of Music We Grew Up Listening To

Something about the meteor bombardment we took last night
and the wild rhythms of destruction
orchestrated like a bassman’s lead. Ba-boom.

People could really sing back then,
when art was considered more precious than gold.
Remember outdoor concerts,
people gathering under smoky skies, and cheering,
alien warfare, fast food, and people still cheered?

And then they made it legal.
Sex, drugs and rock n’ roll, baby.

A Golden Age means books get written about you.
A whole new generation of misfits and lunatics
rose to profit from our accidental snowball house on fire.

I love to sing, but I could never really sing back then
because I always forget the words. Still do.

by Al Simmons

Al Simmons was Poet-in-Residence, City of Chicago, 1979-80.  His show, The Main Event, The World Heavyweight Poetry Championship Fights, highlighted the Taos Poetry Circus, Taos, New Mexico, for twenty years, 1980-2001.  He was nominated for a Rhysling Award.  His work has appeared in 55 magazines and anthologies since 2017, including Abyss & Apex, Cosmic Horror Monthly, Cutleaf, Sci-fi Journal, Merganser Lit, Alien Buddha, Goblins & Galaxies, Scrawl, and Amazing Stories.  He lives in Alameda, CA.
