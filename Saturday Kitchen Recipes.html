The safest single bottle

A fresh, medium‑bodied Pinot Noir – one of Olly Smith’s favourites.

It’s the safest all‑round choice for roast duck, duck breast, Peking duck and duck served with fruit. Its acidity cuts through the fat, while its red‑fruit and earthy flavours complement the rich, slightly gamey meat. Pinot Noir is widely recognised as a classic duck pairing, although the sauce and cooking method still matter.

  • On the 15 Nov 2025 programme, Olly Smith chose Exceptional Central Otago Pinot Noir, New Zealand, 2023 (Asda) to accompany Red wine braised duck legs with prunes, thyme and smashed celeriac.
  • On 05 Apr 2025, he chose Mayu Gran Reserva Pinot Noir, Chile, 2022 (Majestic) with Five‑spice duck with sweet and sour sauce and egg‑fried rice.

Best value, budget and richer‑style picks

Best value pick

Sainsbury’s Taste the Difference Pinot Noir is the most versatile bottle here. It suits roast duck, duck breast, Peking duck and duck with cherry, plum or other fruit‑based sauces.

It has fresh red‑fruit flavours and relatively soft tannins, making it more flexible with duck than a large, heavily oaked red. Independent tasting notes describe it as bright, fruity and food‑friendly.

Best budget pick

Sainsbury’s House Pinot Noir is a sensible everyday choice for roast duck, duck breast and simple pan‑fried duck.

Sainsbury’s lists it as a low‑everyday‑price wine, though the exact price may vary with offers and location. Choose it for a straightforward meal when you want a light, fruity red without spending too much.

Best richer style

Asda’s Penfolds Max’s Shiraz is a good choice for duck confit, grilled duck, five‑spice duck and dishes with a strongly flavoured or savoury sauce.

This full-bodied, structured premium bottle brings bold blackberry fruit and peppery spice, making it the perfect splash-out companion for rich, caramelized, or honey-glazed roast duck.

Alternative premium choice

For a more polished Pinot Noir, choose Oyster Bay Pinot Noir (Asda) if available. Asda describes it with dark cherry, black plum and a silky texture, suitable for roast duck or duck breast.

How to choose in 10 seconds

  • If the dish is rich and fatty (roast duck, confit, grilled or barbecued duck) → go for a red with good acidity: Pinot Noir or Syrah/Shiraz.
  • If there’s a sweet or fruity sauce (orange, cherry, plum, five‑spice, apples) → choose a wine with fruit or a touch of sweetness: off‑dry Riesling, fruity Pinot Noir, Grenache or Pinot Gris.
  • If you’re unsure of the sauce or cooking method → default to a fresh, medium‑bodied Pinot Noir. It’s the safest all‑round choice for most duck dishes.

Use the table at the top for specific matches; use these rules when you’re in a hurry or the menu isn’t clear.

What to avoid

  • Very light, neutral white wines with rich duck – they taste thin beside the meat.
  • Very tannic, heavily oaked reds with delicate duck dishes, especially duck with orange sauce or fruit.
  • With sweet sauces, avoid dry, austere wines that will taste harsh and dry. An off‑dry Riesling is often better than a powerful red for duck à l’orange or hoisin duck.

For Peking duck, Pinot Noir is the familiar red option; Riesling works well if you prefer white.

Serve Pinot Noir slightly cool, not warm. Chill Riesling, rosé and sparkling wine well before serving.

Prices, stock and supermarket offers change, so check the current shelf or online price before buying.
