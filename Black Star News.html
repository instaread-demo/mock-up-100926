Gov. Whitmer And President Grasso: Evidence Too Serious For Silence—Michigan Must Investigate Cassandra Fameux Case

Above: Gov. Whitmer (middle) should learn some lessons from New York’s Gov. Hochul 

Police investigated and recommended a criminal charge. A state regulatory agency reportedly determined that medical records had been altered and described the conduct as criminal. The University of Michigan fired Dr. Paul St. Claire (below) under a nondisclosure agreement (NDA). St. Claire himself testified that the University told him his termination involved a “HIPAA violation.”

While he and his attorney Newberg claim Fameux is “delusional”, court and deposition testimonies raised interesting questions about Dr. St. Claire’s behavior. 

How much more must surface before Michigan demands the truth?

The Cassandra Fameux case has reached the point where silence by Michigan’s most powerful public officials and institutions is increasingly difficult to explain.

Black Star News therefore renews its call to Governor Gretchen Whitmer, Attorney General Dana Nessel, University of Michigan President Domenico Grasso, and the University’s Board of Regents: Do the right thing.

Governor Whitmer should use whatever lawful authority is available to ensure an independent investigation into what happened to Cassandra Fameux, including the conduct of her now ex-husband, Dr. Paul Gregory St. Claire; psychiatrist Dr. Dominic Barberio; University of Michigan Health-Sparrow; and the responses of the institutions that subsequently encountered these allegations.

The University of Michigan should disclose why St. Claire was terminated and explain the circumstances surrounding the nondisclosure agreement executed when his employment ended on February 28, 2024. It should also explain why it never terminated him after substantiating multiple sexual harassment allegations.

This is no longer simply Fameux’s word against her former husband’s. There is a police investigation. There are regulatory records. There are sworn depositions. There are medical records. There is an NDA.

And there are extraordinary contradictions demanding answers. Fameux alleges that St. Claire, an anesthesiologist formerly employed by University of Michigan Health-Sparrow, and psychiatrist Dr. Dominic Barberio “poisoned” her with powerful antipsychotic medications from 2014 through 2023, even though she—and subsequently several psychiatrists—maintained that she did not suffer from schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.

Meridian Township Police investigated Fameux’s complaint for approximately six months. On April 10, 2025, the police report signed by Detective Ian Mandernack concluded: “Forward to the Ingham County Prosecutors Office seeking a charge of domestic assault against Paul Gregory St Claire.”

The prosecutor declined to authorize charges. The controversy surrounding Jane Doe’s allegations at Cornell University has reminded Americans of an important lesson: A prosecutor’s decision not to prosecute does not mean that every legitimate question has been answered—particularly when allegations intersect with powerful institutions such as Cornell University and the University of Michigan.

There is another document Michigan officials cannot responsibly ignore.

Community Mental Health (CMH) social worker Linda Wenzel memorialized information she reported receiving from the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA) in July 2024. According to Wenzel’s note, LARA reported that Fameux’s estranged husband “did in fact access and modify” her records, described the conduct as “criminal” in nature, and recommended that Fameux file a police report and obtain a personal protection order (PPO).

Wenzel subsequently accompanied Fameux when she filed her September 10, 2024 complaint with Meridian Township Police in Okemos, Michigan, leading to the recommendation that a criminal charge be filed against St. Claire.

Separately, in May 2024, LARA spokesperson Abby Rubley told Black Star News that the department did not comment on “open investigations.”

Investigator Norm Naimy later confirmed to this newspaper in November 2024 that an investigation remained active. So what did LARA ultimately determine? LARA Director Marlon I. Brown, a Whitmer appointee, has not responded to Black Star News inquiries. The public has a right to know. If the agency found alleged “criminal” behavior and as memorialized by Wenzel, why does St. Claire still hold a medical license? 

LARA Director Marlon I. Brown. If the agency found alleged “criminal” behavior and as memorialized by Wenzel, why does St. Claire still hold a medical license? 

When University of Michigan Health-Sparrow terminated St. Claire on February 28, 2024, why did it enter into a nondisclosure agreement with him?

That question has become far more important because of St. Claire’s own sworn testimony and the police recommendation of a criminal charge.

During his September 25, 2024 divorce deposition, Fameux’s attorney, Timothy Young, questioned St. Claire about whether there was evidence in his Sparrow employment record establishing that Black Star News reporting had caused his termination.

St. Claire responded: “Yeah, I’m not privy to that other than—all I know is they said HIPAA violation. Your wife states that.”

Yet St. Claire, in what Black Star News considers an ongoing campaign to muzzle investigative journalism, sued the newspaper, its publisher Milton Allimadi, and Fameux for defamation in July 2024, claiming, among other things, that the reporting damaged his reputation and employment.

Two years later, in July 2026, after Black Star News served interrogatory responses 48 hours late, St. Claire obtained a default and a multimillion-dollar jury award from a Michigan court. The 48 hours delay occurred after Allimadi discovered that years’ worth of email messages exchanged with St. Claire’s lawyers had been deleted in an apparent hacking incident, necessitating many hours of searches and causing delays. The incident was reported to the FBI and NYPD.

Allimadi has already moved to set aside the default and resulting judgment.

The timing of St. Claire’s 2024 lawsuit also deserves scrutiny.

According to Wenzel’s contemporaneous note, July 2024 was the same month LARA informed her that St. Claire had accessed and modified Fameux’s records and characterized the conduct as criminal.

Was the timing of the lawsuit coincidental? The first Black Star News article detailing Fameux’s allegations was in December 2023 and St. Claire did not sue until seven months later. That is precisely why Michigan needs an independent investigation rather than assumptions.

Here are questions for President Grasso and the University of Michigan:

President Grasso evidently believes the football program takes priority over Fameux’s case.

What was the HIPAA violation to which St. Claire testified? Did it involve Cassandra Fameux? Did it involve access to or alteration of her medical records? Why did the University execute an NDA with St. Claire when it terminated him?

Fameux alleges that she received Invega Sustenna injections between approximately 2017 and 2021 without a legitimate schizophrenia diagnosis. She maintains that no valid prescription supported the years-long course of injections.

In fact, on December 16, 2014, around the time Fameux alleges that St. Claire and Barberio began “poisoning” her with antipsychotic medications, Dr. John Maino documented the following diagnoses: anorexia; depression; anxiety; anxiety attacks; insomnia; and microcytic hypochromic anemia.

There was no mention of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.

In divorce testimony, St. Claire characterized his administration of the injections as occurring only “occasionally.”

Yet, in his February 7, 2025 deposition, Barberio testified that St. Claire personally administered approximately 90 percent of Fameux’s Invega Sustenna injections, while Barberio’s nurses administered approximately 10 percent in his office.

Detective Mandernack’s police report states that Barberio produced documentation for only one prescription despite nearly four years of injections.

Fameux also provided police with a secretly recorded September 2023 conversation in which Barberio acknowledged that she was not schizophrenic.

Invega Sustenna is a powerful antipsychotic medication with potentially serious adverse effects. Fameux attributes several serious medical conditions to the years of injections: a brain tumor; a heart condition requiring implantation of a loop monitor in her chest; diabetes; and infertility following surgery to stop excessive menstrual bleeding. These alleged causal connections require independent medical examination.

Then there is the 2018 Judgment of Separate Maintenance (JOSM), which Fameux and her divorce attorney, Timothy Young, said she signed while under the influence of antipsychotic medications. Fameux signed the agreement on February 20, 2018, transferring 100 percent of the marital assets—millions of dollars and two homes—to St. Claire.

St. Claire had successfully moved the Ingham County Circuit Court to declare Fameux legally incompetent in 2015. Yet the court never appointed a guardian ad litem (GAL) for Fameux before she signed the JOSM, Black Star News reported. Black Star News also reported in November 2025 allegations that St. Claire, his divorce attorney Jessica Larson, and Judge Carol Koenig, who presided over the case, participated in fabricating the existence of a GAL. The alleged fabrication helped sustain the JOSM, according to the reporting.

These allegations concern proceedings in the same Lansing, Michigan, court system where St. Claire’s defamation case is being heard. They warrant independent examination.

Six months after that reporting, Larson departed her law firm, and shortly thereafter, the firm, Mallory Lapka Scott Selin, apparently shut down.

Yet the JOSM remains in effect. Fameux says she has been left largely dependent on retirement funds and without health insurance, preventing her from having her brain tumor regularly monitored.

St. Claire, in his defamation case, claims that Black Star News reporting was based on Fameux’s “delusions.”

In a July 22, 2026 Facebook posting attacking Black Star News and Allimadi, St. Claire’s attorney, C. Christopher Newberg, characterized the journalism as a “relentless, vindictive, and malicious online smear campaign” and a “delusional web of heinous, fabricated criminal accusations.” He also referred to supposedly “bizarre” and “fictional” claims.

Yet during St. Claire’s 2003 divorce proceedings, attorney Nan Elizabeth Casey, representing his late second wife, Dr. Marcy Street, stated that evidence would be introduced that St. Claire had previously spent more than three weeks in a mental-health institution.

During that same divorce from Dr. Street—whom St. Claire also accused of mental illness, although he qualified that characterization in a 2024 divorce deposition by saying she was not “crazy crazy” like Fameux—the couple’s household employee, Shelley Van Epps, filed a sworn affidavit dated December 4, 2003, containing disturbing allegations.

Van Epps alleged that on November 13, at 11 am, she found St. Claire eating cat food while drinking wine. She claimed that St. Claire told her “he was horny” and asked whether she was horny, too. She alleged that he asked whether her husband “rubbed her clitoris.”

She further alleged that St. Claire asked whether she believed her husband would “kill her and her children.” Van Epps concluded her affidavit: “I was frightened by his behavior which I found bizarre and unexplainable… That I called Dr. Street and explained what had happened for reason that I was fearful of his behavior and afraid for Dr. Street and her children.”

Black Star News does not present these allegations as a psychiatric diagnosis or as proof of any medical condition.

Years later, on October 2, 2024, during his divorce deposition, Fameux’s attorney, Young, questioned St. Claire about Van Epps’ allegations. According to the deposition transcript page 47, St. Claire responded: “Yeah, but anyhow, I remember her saying that I ate dog food. I don’t deny that I tried dog food a couple times, a little piece of it, and when she asked me why, I said because I’m hungry, and maybe what she thought I said was that ‘I’m horny.’ I’d joke and say ‘hungry,’ but she might interpret it as ‘horny.’ Yes, sir, I have tried my dog food to see what it tastes like, a little pellet. I don’t deny that. I don’t eat it for lunch.”

While he and his attorney Newberg claim Fameux is “delusional”, court and deposition testimonies raised interesting questions about Dr. St. Claire’s behavior. 

That testimony speaks for itself. Black Star News does not need to embellish it.

If St. Claire’s attorney wants the public to judge Allimadi’s journalism by tossing around words like “delusional” and “bizarre,” and if St. Claire continues invoking Fameux’s alleged mental illness to undermine her credibility, then the public deserves to know that court records contain allegations and testimony concerning St. Claire himself that might readily have been subjected to the same kind of irresponsible rhetoric.

The point is not to diagnose St. Claire. It is to expose the glaring double standard in the use of psychiatric labels to discredit a Haitian immigrant woman whose allegations have generated a police investigation, a recommendation for criminal charge, regulatory scrutiny, and substantial sworn testimony.

The ball is now in Governor Whitmer’s and University of Michigan President Grasso’s court. Perhaps both hoped St. Claire’s defamation case would distract attention from the Fameux case and what happened to her.

If so, they miscalculated. The Cornell case has redirected national attention toward the accountability of powerful universities and the institutions charged with investigating allegations against people associated with them.

Michigan cannot credibly demand accountability elsewhere while refusing to confront the troubling questions arising within its own institutions.

The police investigated. The police recommended a criminal charge. A state regulatory agency reportedly identified potentially criminal conduct involving medical records. The University terminated St. Claire under an NDA. And St. Claire’s own sworn testimony raises questions about the reason for his dismissal.

How much more evidence must emerge before Michigan’s leaders act?

In Michigan, the chickens have come home to roost.

Governor Whitmer and President Grasso—Do the right thing.
